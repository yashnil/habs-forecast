# CoastWatch "My Coast": who fishes where, what they decide, and what they need

*Desk research, 12 Oct 2026. Every factual claim has a URL. **[D]** marks a need documented in a published source. **[H]** marks a hypothesis that needs stakeholder interviews before it shapes the product. Status statements about current closures are as fetched on 12 Oct 2026 and will go stale. They illustrate the kinds of notice CoastWatch must handle; they are not content to reuse.*

## 0. The regulatory frame every user group operates in (documented)

| Instrument | Who decides | What it means | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fishery closure / delayed opening** (FGC §5523) | OEHHA Director, consulting CDPH, *recommends*. CDFW Director *orders*. CDFW then asks the Fish and Game Commission for a public discussion. | Take and possession are prohibited in a defined area. Commercial, recreational, or both. | [CDFW health advisories](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Fishing/Ocean/Health-Advisories); [CDFW 2024 rec. crab delay](https://wildlife.ca.gov/News/Archive/recreational-dungeness-crab-fishery-delayed-in-a-portion-of-northern-california-state-waters-due-to-public-health-hazard); [OEHHA 2016 reopening](https://oehha.ca.gov/fish/general-info/recreational-dungeness-crab-fishery-open-south-sonomamendocino-county-line) |

| **Crab action levels** | CDPH (draft regulation) | Viscera >30 ppm fails. Meat ≥20 ppm fails. Six crabs per site set. Meat is tested if any viscera fails. If meat fails, OEHHA is consulted. | [CDPH DPH‑24‑004 text](https://cdph.ca.gov/Programs/OLS/CDPH%20Document%20Library/DPH-24-004_Text.pdf) |

Example: "do not eat the internal organs" and "cooking does not destroy the toxin". | [CDPH SN26‑003](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/OPA/Pages/SN26-003.aspx); [2017 lobster notice](https://socalfishreports.com/fish_reports/128422/commercial-spiny-lobster-fishery-closed-due-to-public-health-hazard.php) |

**Geography.** Since 2020 the state has managed crab DA through a fixed set of **biotoxin management areas (BMAs)**, intended to make closures more predictable. Pomeroy & Culver (2026) count eight; a CDFW 2020 presentation counted nine. Many BMAs span two counties. BMA H coincides with the southern rock crab permit area ([Pomeroy & Culver 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1881090/abstract); [CDFW to DCTF 2020](https://www.opc.ca.gov/webmaster/_media_library/2009/04/CDFW-Updates-to-DCTF-2020_10202020.pdf)). Notices define areas by landmarks and latitude/longitude lines, not ports. Examples: the 2024 recreational delay ran from the Oregon line south to the Reading Rock SMR boundary at 41°17.6′N, in **state waters** only ([CDFW](https://wildlife.ca.gov/News/Archive/recreational-dungeness-crab-fishery-delayed-in-a-portion-of-northern-california-state-waters-due-to-public-health-hazard)); the 2017 lobster closure covered state waters out to 3 nmi around Santa Cruz and Anacapa islands between two meridians ([notice](https://socalfishreports.com/fish_reports/128422/commercial-spiny-lobster-fishery-closed-due-to-public-health-hazard.php)).

**Two distinctions that need expert review**, because the product must not paraphrase them wrongly:
- **State vs federal waters.** Several notices are explicitly "state waters" only. How each action applies seaward of 3 nmi, and to vessels landing in California, is a legal question.
- **The Tri‑State meat‑quality process** is separate from DA testing. WDFW, ODFW and CDFW run a coastwide test fishery from Point Arena to Canada. Minimum meat recovery is 23–24%, and opening zones follow "traditional fishing patterns" ([Tri‑State protocol](https://dfw.state.or.us/agency/commission/minutes/19/09_Sep/B/Attachment%206_Tri-State%20Protocol.pdf); [ODFW 2024 pre‑season protocols](https://www.dfw.state.or.us/mrp/shellfish/commercial/crab/docs/2024/Pre-Season%20Testing%20Protocols%202024_FINAL.pdf)). In 2025–26 California "was unable to conduct any meat yield testing due to high biotoxin levels" ([ODFW/Tri‑State results](https://dfw.state.or.us/MRP/shellfish/commercial/crab/docs/2025/Tri-StatePreseasonCoastwideResults_2025_Rnd3.pdf)). Delayed areas also trigger a **30‑day Fair Start** rule ([CDFW](https://wildlife.ca.gov/News/Archive/commercial-dungeness-crab-fishery-to-open-in-portions-of-northern-california-one-area-further-delayed-due-to-public-health-hazard)). Whale‑entanglement rules (RAMP) also delay openers ([Pomeroy & Culver 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1881090/abstract)).

**Official channels.**
- CDFW health‑advisory page, news releases and Marine Region blog.
- CDFW DA Closure Information Line: (831) 649‑2883.
- CDPH Biotoxin line: (800) 553‑4133.
- CDPH crab analytical‑data tables.
- PSMFC "Commercial Dungeness Crab News" for tri‑state industry notices.

Sources: [CDFW](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Fishing/Ocean/Health-Advisories); [PSMFC](https://old.psmfc.org/crab/). PCFFA's crab page compiles *season information flyers* and whale‑safe/RAMP material, not DA data ([PCFFA](https://www.pcffa.org/crab-resources)).

## 1. Commercial fishermen (Dungeness crab, rock crab, wetfish, spiny lobster)

### Documented [D]

**The stakes are concentrated in time.**
- In 2015–16, California crabbers lost up to **72% of the season** ([Moore et al. 2020 Frontiers, citing Moore et al. 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2019.00803/full)). The opening was delayed by up to five months ([NOAA Fisheries](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/west-coast/science-data/effects-harmful-algal-blooms-west-coast-fishing-communities)).
- Most revenue comes in the first 6–8 weeks of the season, around the holidays ([Marine Fish Conservation Network](https://conservefish.org/2016/05/26/stumbling-through-disaster/)). Closures "occurred shortly before the winter holiday season" ([Pomeroy & Culver 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1881090/abstract)).
- Closure timing during peak season amplified losses ([Moore et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2019.00803/full)).

**The harm is port‑specific.**
- Crescent City, Fort Bragg and Moss Landing were the most socially vulnerable and crab‑dependent communities, and lost the most crab fishing opportunity ([Moore et al. 2019, *Marine Policy*](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/west-coast/science-data/effects-harmful-algal-blooms-west-coast-fishing-communities)).
- In Crescent City, the hardship spread to hospitality and retail. Interviewees reported barriers to accessing assistance ([Ritzman et al. 2018](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/62735)).
- Small and large vessels were affected differently ([Jardine et al. 2020](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/west-coast/science-data/effects-harmful-algal-blooms-west-coast-fishing-communities)).

**Key decisions: where to fish and land, and whether to move.**
- Under area closures, "only mobile fishermen and local fishermen regained access," which sometimes caused conflict over fishing grounds ([Pomeroy & Culver 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1881090/abstract)).
- Fishing an alternate fishery raised the probability of recovering income by 0.28 for fishers. A side job did not help fishers ([Moore et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2019.00803/full)). An "alternate fisheries" view per port is therefore decision‑relevant.

**Questions fishermen ask the system.** These come from the 2016 legislative hearing and DCTF material:
- They want "written and enforceable protocols for domoic acid testing… timing, notification, and opening procedures."
- They want coordinated consumer messaging that tested crab is safe.
- They question the 30 ppm threshold and the 30‑day fair start rule.

Sources: [CWPA account of 28 Apr 2016 hearing](https://californiawetfish.org/cwpa-blog/2016/05/03/one-north-coast-hot-crab-pushes-californias-fishery-officials-to-reconsider-opening-protocols); [hearing agenda](https://fisheries.legislature.ca.gov/sites/fisheries.legislature.ca.gov/files/u8/2016-4-28%20public%20agenda%20LL.pdf). A single "hot crab" at Trinidad kept the North Coast closed in April 2016 (same source).

**Evisceration is not a neutral fix.** It favours the fishermen and few buyers who have the capacity to eviscerate. Most rock crab handlers lack that capacity, rock crab is sold mostly live, and some buyers reject eviscerated product ([Pomeroy & Culver 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1881090/abstract)). Processors may eviscerate pre‑emptively on rumour ([PSMFC 2016 CA protocols](https://www.psmfc.org/crab/2016-2017%20files/calif%20protocols.pdf)).

**Testing capacity limits granularity.** The state has one lab and testing costs about $100 per crab. Better testing "could enable smaller closures, but could also lead to more closures" ([Pomeroy & Culver 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1881090/abstract)). Six crabs per site gives high statistical power, and denser sampling lets managers respond faster with less disruption to fishing ([Free, Moore & Trainer 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35550293/)).

**Fishers already watch models.** A Dungeness fisher is quoted: "we are following these models daily" ([Kudela, Anderson & Ruhl 2021, *Oceanography*](https://tos.org/oceanography/article/the-california-harmful-algal-bloom-monitoring-and-alert-program-a-success-story-for-coordinated-ocean-observing)).

**Other fisheries face real, less‑publicised actions.**
- Commercial spiny lobster was closed around Anacapa and east Santa Cruz Island from Oct 2017 to Jan 2018. The action level is 20 ppm in viscera. The recreational fishery stayed open with a "don't eat tomalley" warning ([closure](https://socalfishreports.com/fish_reports/128422/commercial-spiny-lobster-fishery-closed-due-to-public-health-hazard.php); [lift](https://socalfishreports.com/fish_reports/130600/commercial-spiny-lobster-fishery-closure-lifted-near-santa-cruz-and-anacapa-islands.php)). That is one event with two different rules for two user groups.
- Anchovy and sardine were restricted in Monterey Bay ([CDFW](https://wildlife.ca.gov/News/Archive/monterey-bay-northern-anchovy-and-pacific-sardine-commercial-take-restrictions-and-recreational-advisory-in-place-due-to-public-health-hazard)).
- Rock crab commercial closures and viscera warnings run on separate geographies from Dungeness ([CDFW](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Fishing/Ocean/Health-Advisories)).

### Hypotheses [H]
- H1. The unit of decision is **port plus the BMA(s) reachable from it**, not a grid cell. Fishermen want "what is the status of every area I could fish or land in from port X," including adjacent BMAs and Fair Start consequences.
- H2. Before an opener, the most valued item is **where the next test round stands**: which BMA, when the last sample was taken, and when the earliest possible clearance is, given the two‑clean‑sets ≥7 days rule. Ocean forecasts matter less.
- H3. Commercial fishers trust fleet channels (PSMFC notices, CDFW industry notices, association texts, buyers) more than websites. A site is useful only if it is faster or easier to scan than these channels, and it must never contradict them.
- H4. A probability forecast that can be read as "this area will close" could be seen as harmful to markets (see §6). Some fishers may oppose its prominence.
- H5. Wetfish and lobster operators know far less about the HAB risk to their fisheries than crabbers do, and would value a per‑species status line.

## 2. Recreational fishermen and CPFV (party/charter) boats

### Documented [D]
- Recreational and commercial status often **diverge**. Examples: the 2017 lobster case above; the recreational crab season opening on time in some areas while the commercial opener was delayed for meat quality and whales ([CDFW](https://wildlife.ca.gov/News/Archive/recreational-dungeness-crab-fishery-open-in-northern-california-continued-meat-quality-and-whale-protection-delays-for-commercial-fishery)); and the 2024 recreational delay limited to state waters ([CDFW](https://wildlife.ca.gov/News/Archive/recreational-dungeness-crab-fishery-delayed-in-a-portion-of-northern-california-state-waters-due-to-public-health-hazard)).
- Open‑but‑advised areas carry instructions anglers must act on: discard viscera and cooking liquid, and clean crab before cooking ([CDPH SN26‑003](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/OPA/Pages/SN26-003.aspx); [CDFW 2025 rec. opening](https://wildlife.ca.gov/News/Archive/recreational-dungeness-crab-fishery-to-open-in-del-norte-mendocino-and-parts-of-humboldt-counties-after-delay-due-to-public-health-risks)).
- Recreational closures come with transit and possession allowances set in a Director's Declaration ([CDFW](https://wildlife.ca.gov/News/Archive/recreational-dungeness-crab-fishery-delayed-in-a-portion-of-northern-california-state-waters-due-to-public-health-hazard)).
- Restrictions are species‑specific. The Monterey anchovy and sardine action did not affect rockfish, halibut, salmon and similar species, which is a key reassurance for a party boat ([CDFW](https://wildlife.ca.gov/News/Archive/monterey-bay-northern-anchovy-and-pacific-sardine-commercial-take-restrictions-and-recreational-advisory-in-place-due-to-public-health-hazard)).

### Hypotheses [H]
- H6. The recreational question is literal: "Can I keep crab, lobster or bait I catch out of harbor X this weekend, and what must I do before eating it?" That calls for a species‑by‑species status for the launch point, with the official link and the hotline.
- H7. CPFV operators field customer questions and want a shareable, authoritative‑sourced status card for their port.
- H8. Many recreational crabbers do not distinguish closures from advisories. *No California survey of crabber advisory awareness was found*, so this is a research gap.

## 3. Shellfish harvesters

### Recreational [D]
- The mussel quarantine is statewide and seasonal, but dates can change, and **separate county or species advisories run all year**. Examples include razor clams in Humboldt and bivalves around the Northern Channel Islands ([SLO County](https://www.slocounty.ca.gov/departments/health-agency/public-health/environmental-health-services/division-articles/annual-quarantine-of-sport-harvested-mussels-ends-october-31); [Ventura County 2024](https://news.venturacounty.gov/en/20240502-press-release-2/)).
- Razor clam fisheries close by county. On the fetch date, Humboldt had been closed since May 2024 and Del Norte closed on 9 Oct 2026 ([CDFW](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Fishing/Ocean/Health-Advisories)).
- The PSP and DA risk includes symptoms within 30 minutes to 24 hours, toxins that cooking does not reliably destroy, and no antidote ([CDPH](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CEH/DRSEM/Pages/EMB/Shellfish/Annual-Mussel-Quarantine.aspx)). **PSP is out of CoastWatch's data scope.** A "My Coast" shellfish view built on DA and Pseudo‑nitzschia data alone would therefore be misleadingly incomplete.

### Commercial aquaculture [D]
- Certified growers are bound by CDPH weekly sampling. Advisories for recreational harvest do not apply to their product ([CalCOFI](https://calcofi.com/publications/calcofireports/v54/Vol_54_Carter_75-79.pdf); [CDPH SGA](https://www.casqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/item_04d_-_cdph_-_introduction_to_classifying_shellfish_growing_areas_090722_vzw.pdf)).
- Growers are cited as routine users of the real‑time model ([OPC 2016 update](https://opc.ca.gov/webmaster/ftp/pdf/agenda_items/20160203/Item6_HarmfulAlgalBloomUpdate.pdf)) and as wanting early warning ([Anderson, US CLIVAR](https://usclivar.org/sites/default/files/documents/Andersonv2-Session2.pdf)).
- But C‑HARM is "not good at predicting estuarine toxicity," and shellfish toxicity is "often decoupled" from it ([EPA archive, CA HAB risk](https://archive.epa.gov/epa/sites/production/files/2017-05/documents/california-harmful-algae-risk-r9.pdf)). Growers sit in bays such as Tomales, Humboldt and Morro, exactly where the 3 km grid is weakest.
- Lead‑time evidence comes from outside California. European fish and shellfish farmers said a **2–3 day** warning would be a major benefit ([CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/article/id/90329-new-tool-to-predict-algal-blooms)). A Maine site‑specific shellfish forecast at **weekly** range was co‑designed with growers ([NCCOS](https://coastalscience.noaa.gov/?p=29366)).

### Hypotheses [H]
- H9. Growers' marginal use of CoastWatch is anticipatory: deciding when to pull extra samples or hold harvest. That needs site‑relevant (bay) signals, which CoastWatch does not have.
- H10. Recreational clammers need a beach‑level "closed, advisory or quarantine" lookup more than any ocean product.

## 4. Port managers, harbor districts, processors and buyers

### Documented [D]
- Port‑level losses spill into hospitality and retail ([Ritzman et al. 2018](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/62735)). In Washington's analogous razor clam case, a full‑season closure at one town cost local businesses at least about $0.84 M ([Weir, Kourantidou & Jin, via NOAA repository](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/57794/noaa_57794_DS1.pdf)). The PNW HAB Bulletin is described as a way for businesses to hedge (same source).
- Processors: EO compliance requires Approved Processor status. Ports without processing capacity are disadvantaged ([CDPH FAQ](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CEH/DFDCS/Pages/FDBPrograms/FoodSafetyProgram/CrabEviscerationFAQ.aspx); [Pomeroy & Culver 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1881090/abstract)). In 2015–16, handlers' supply "stopped abruptly" (same source).
- Brand and market confidence drive decisions. The industry backed a statewide rather than spot closure to protect the California Dungeness reputation. A processor: "If we only have a pocket of crabs that are safe, that is probably not going to give much confidence to the public" ([MFCN](https://conservefish.org/2016/05/26/stumbling-through-disaster/)). Federal relief arrived about four years late and "could not make them whole" ([Pomeroy & Culver 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1881090/abstract)).
- Disaster‑declaration paths (Fishery Disaster, HHENS) depend on documented impacts ([Duke MP](https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/items/1e1fca34-408e-43d5-bec2-de5214dc5f24); [Ekstrom et al. 2020](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/62734)).

### Hypotheses [H]
- H11. Harbor districts want a port page showing what is closed, the official links, and historical landings by species. They would use it for public questions, council briefings and disaster documentation. Historical landings by port, which CoastWatch already has, may be its most distinctive asset for this group.
- H12. Processors and buyers care about adjacent‑area status and EO boundaries more than ocean conditions.

## 5. HAB researchers and monitoring organisations

### Documented [D]
- CalHABMAP shore‑station data are served via the SCCOOS ERDDAP. Metadata are inconsistent across data.ioos.us, data.gov and ERDDAP, and SPATT toxin data is catalogued separately ([data.ioos.us](https://data.ioos.us/dataset/calhabmap-habs-humboldt-data); [ERDDAP](https://erddap.sccoos.org/erddap/tabledap/HABs-HumboldtSouthBay.fgdc)).
- The CA IFCB network has 12 instruments, ML classification, and a dashboard at ifcb.caloos.org ([UCSD Library](https://library.ucsd.edu/dc/collection/bb38016322); [SCCWRP](https://www.sccwrp.org/news/statewide-habs-early-warning-system-being-developed/)).
- Program leads stress standardised methods, products that end users can interpret, coverage beyond a few stations, and a scope beyond Pseudo‑nitzschia ([Kudela et al. 2021](https://tos.org/oceanography/article/the-california-harmful-algal-bloom-monitoring-and-alert-program-a-success-story-for-coordinated-ocean-observing)).
- The PNW analogue: the bulletin helped managers decide on sampling and on harvest openings, extensions and closures. Crab DA lags razor clam DA by "a couple of weeks" ([McCabe, Hickey & Trainer 2023](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/56421)). That lag is a useful lead‑time principle, but it is not a validated California rule.
- Simulation and power analysis are underused tools for designing monitoring ([Free et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35550293/)).

### Hypotheses [H]
- H13. Researchers want provenance‑first downloads with original units, sample versus report dates, and links back to ERDDAP. They also want a co‑located time series per site (shore DA and PN, C‑HARM at the nearest valid cell, chlorophyll) rather than a new forecast.
- H14. Monitoring organisations would value seeing where users look but data are missing. This is a gap map.

## 6. Documented pitfalls and harms

1. **Advisory, closure and quarantine confusion.**
   - One event can produce a commercial closure, a recreational advisory and an unaffected neighbouring fishery at the same time ([lobster 2017](https://socalfishreports.com/fish_reports/128422/commercial-spiny-lobster-fishery-closed-due-to-public-health-hazard.php)).
   - Recreational advisories do not apply to certified commercial shellfish ([Monterey Co.](https://countyofmonterey.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/121751/638340007368370000)).
   - A single "risk colour" collapses these distinctions.
2. **Messaging that reduces demand broadly.** Advisories can depress demand for *all* crab, not just the viscera ([Pomeroy & Culver 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1881090/abstract)). Consumer confidence lingered low after reopening ([MFCN](https://conservefish.org/2016/05/26/stumbling-through-disaster/)). A public "high DA risk" map near a port during an open season could cause this same harm.
3. **Mistrust of thresholds and testing.** Some participants questioned whether the risk is overstated and whether the foil‑wrap testing method inflates DA ([Pomeroy & Culver 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1881090/abstract)). Panelists asked for research into the 30 ppm threshold ([CWPA](https://californiawetfish.org/cwpa-blog/2016/05/03/one-north-coast-hot-crab-pushes-californias-fishery-officials-to-reconsider-opening-protocols)). A third party that appears to endorse or contradict agencies will be pulled into this dispute.
4. **Process opacity.** Fishermen asked for written protocols covering timing and notification, and for more transparency in decision‑making ([CWPA](https://californiawetfish.org/cwpa-blog/2016/05/03/one-north-coast-hot-crab-pushes-californias-fishery-officials-to-reconsider-opening-protocols); [Pomeroy & Culver 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1881090/abstract)). *Explaining the process* is a documented need. *Predicting outcomes* is not.
5. **Lag.** In a freshwater HAB programme, state signs lagged testing. Evaluated messages sometimes "provide information for scientists and not the public" ([J. Environ. Health 2023](https://pubs.neha.org/view/421614739/26/); [Hardy et al. 2016](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/30786)). These findings are freshwater, so the analogy is imperfect. CalHABMAP results also arrive days to weeks after sampling, so stale data must look stale.
6. **Model scope.** C‑HARM is weak in estuaries and decoupled from shellfish toxicity ([EPA archive](https://archive.epa.gov/epa/sites/production/files/2017-05/documents/california-harmful-algae-risk-r9.pdf)). It covers Pseudo‑nitzschia only, not PSP ([Kudela et al. 2021](https://tos.org/oceanography/article/the-california-harmful-algal-bloom-monitoring-and-alert-program-a-success-story-for-coordinated-ocean-observing)).
7. **The economics of false alarms.** Targeted forecasts reduce false alarms and unnecessary costs ([NCCOS](https://coastalscience.noaa.gov/project/harmful-algal-bloom-hab-forecasting/)). Forecast value depends on accuracy and on how effective responses are ([Gulf of Maine valuation](https://darchive.mblwhoilibrary.org/entities/publication/3aa33bec-5ac4-52b0-b1c9-0b6e10ac54b1)). Untargeted public alarms carry costs for a fleet that earns most of its revenue in 6–8 weeks.
8. **Distributional harm.** EO and zone‑based openings favour mobile vessels and ports with processing capacity ([Pomeroy & Culver 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1881090/abstract)). A feature that helps mobile boats find open areas can intensify gear conflict ([same](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1881090/abstract)).

## 7. What CoastWatch can reliably provide vs what needs experts or new data

**Can provide now (with care)**
- **Port/location page as an index to official status.** For each species and each user type (commercial, recreational, shellfish), show the latest transcribed CDFW/CDPH notice, its effective date, the "last checked" time, and a deep link plus the hotline numbers.
  - The page should say "see official notice" and *never* "open" or "safe".
  - It must show "no notice found" distinctly from "no restriction".
- **Process explainer per port.** List which BMA(s) the port sits in and what the reopen rule is (two clean sets ≥7 days apart). This explains, with citations, rather than predicts.
- **Observation context.** The nearest CalHABMAP stations, with sample date vs report date and an age badge; satellite chlorophyll labelled "chlorophyll, not toxin"; HF‑radar currents.
- **C‑HARM context.** The nearest *valid* offshore cell, with its distance shown explicitly, labelled as a regional model probability of Pseudo‑nitzschia/DA conditions. It is not a forecast for the harbor and says nothing about shellfish or crab safety.
- **Historical landings by port and species.** This supports harbor districts and disaster documentation (H11).

**Needs regulatory expertise**
- Mapping notice text (landmarks and lat/long lines) to polygons and to ports.
- State vs federal waters applicability.
- EO implications for a given landing.
- Fair Start consequences.
- Director's Declaration transit and possession allowances.
- Whether rec and commercial rules differ for a species and area.

All of these need CDFW, CDPH or OEHHA review, or should be quoted verbatim with a link rather than interpreted.

**Needs new data or partners**
- PSP data, which is entirely absent.
- CDPH crab analytical results by BMA. Ingestion would be useful (H2), but needs CDPH agreement on presentation.
- Bay and estuary toxin signals for growers.
- IFCB feeds.
- Tri‑State/PSMFC notices.
- Validated crab‑lag relationships for California.

## 8. Interview and validation plan

**Who to interview (about 15–20 people, stratified North, Central and South Coast):**
- Commercial Dungeness crabbers (Crescent City, Trinidad/Eureka, Fort Bragg, Bodega, SF/Half Moon Bay), including small and large vessels.
- Rock crab and lobster permittees (Santa Barbara/Ventura).
- Wetfish: California Wetfish Producers Association.
- PCFFA, Humboldt Fishermen's Marketing Association, and Crescent City Commercial Fishermen's Association.
- California Dungeness Crab Task Force (DCTF) members and its administrative team (OPC).
- Harbor districts: Crescent City Harbor District, Humboldt Bay Harbor District, Noyo Harbor District, Santa Barbara Waterfront.
- One or two processors and buyers, including an Approved Processor.
- CPFV operators (Bodega, Monterey, Morro Bay, Santa Barbara).
- Recreational crab and clam clubs.
- Shellfish growers: Tomales Bay, Humboldt Bay, Morro Bay, and the California Aquaculture Association.
- California Sea Grant fisheries extension (North Coast and Central Coast advisors).
- Researchers: CalHABMAP, CeNCOOS/SCCOOS, UCSC (Kudela; Pomeroy, whose 2026 study already interviewed this population).
- *Informational only, not for endorsement:* CDFW Marine Region, CDPH Marine Biotoxin and Food & Drug Branch, and OEHHA staff, to confirm wording and boundaries.

**Questions (8–12)**
1. Walk me through the last time a domoic acid notice affected you. How did you first hear, how long after it was issued, and what did you do next?
2. Before choosing where to fish, harvest or land this season, what do you check, in what order, and which source do you trust most? Why?
3. Which areas or ports do you consider "yours," and how far would you move if your area closed?
4. How much warning would have changed a decision (gear, crew, buyer, trip, travel)? What would you have done differently with 3 days, 2 weeks, or a month of warning?
5. In your own words, how do a closure, a health advisory, an evisceration order and the mussel quarantine differ? *(Comprehension check; record errors.)*
6. If a public website showed "elevated bloom risk" off your port while the season is open, what would happen with buyers, customers and the public? Is that helpful or harmful?
7. What about the testing and opening process is hardest to follow (sites, BMAs, two clean tests, Fair Start, Tri‑State meat fill)?
8. Show the prototype port page. What is wrong, missing or misleading? What would make you stop using it?
9. Would you share this page with crew, customers or council? What would it need for you to do that?
10. *(Researchers and harbor staff)* Which data, formats and provenance details would make this page citable in your work or reports?
11. *(Growers)* Does an offshore model or pier station say anything useful about your bay? What would?
12. Who else should we talk to?

**Success metrics for "My Coast"**
- **Comprehension (primary safety metric):**
  - At least 90% of tested users correctly state that CoastWatch does *not* declare an area open or safe.
  - At least 80% correctly classify a shown status as closure, advisory, evisceration order or quarantine, measured in task‑based tests before and after the redesign.
- **No‑harm:**
  - Zero instances in testing where a user infers "safe to harvest" from the absence of a notice or from a low model value.
  - Agency reviewers sign off on status wording.
- **Correctness and freshness:**
  - 100% of displayed notices link to a live official source.
  - Median lag from official publication to CoastWatch display is under 24 h.
  - Stale‑data badges appear on every observation older than its threshold.
- **Usefulness:**
  - At least 70% of fishermen and harbor interviewees say the port page saves time against their current routine.
  - Task time to "find the official status for species X at port Y" is at least 50% faster than the baseline using agency sites.
  - Repeat weekly use by the pilot cohort through one crab opener.
- **Trust:**
  - Users rate the page "accurate and not alarmist" at 4 or more out of 5.
  - No complaints from the fleet or processors that the site depressed demand.

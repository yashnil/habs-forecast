# CoastWatch: Stakeholder Research Synthesis (California HAB information users)

*Desk research only, compiled 2026-10-12. No one was contacted and no interviews were run. Everything below comes from published sources or is labeled as an assumption.*

---

## 0. How to read this report

**Evidence labels used throughout**

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[E]** | Evidence-backed: directly supported by a cited published study, agency document or official page. Source IDs (S#) are listed in section 11. |
| **[E-adj]** | Adjacent evidence: supported by a study of a neighbouring population (Oregon or Washington fishers, Florida red tide, Lake Erie, national angler survey). The inference to the California group is plausible but not yet shown. |
| **[A]** | Assumption or hypothesis. It is reasonable but unpublished, so it must be tested in the interviews and usability study (sections 6–8). |
| **[UNVERIFIED]** | A factual claim taken from a search snippet or secondary summary that I could not confirm against the primary text. |

**Snapshot caveat.** The "current status" items, such as the September 2026 anchovy closure and the October 2026 Del Norte razor clam closure, come from the CDFW Health Advisories page as fetched on 2026-10-12 [S12]. They are illustrations of the regulatory landscape. They are not a feed, and they will go stale.

**Method.** I searched and read peer-reviewed socioeconomic HAB studies of the 2015–16 West Coast domoic acid (DA) event. I also read California agency pages (CDFW, CDPH, OEHHA), the Tri-State testing protocol documents, the SCCOOS CA HAB Bulletin page, and adjacent forecast-use studies. Full text was read for S2, S3, S5, S6, S7 and S9. For S8 I read long verbatim excerpts. For S1, S10 and S16 I read only the abstracts or summaries.

## 1. The evidence base in one page

| ID | Source | What it tells us (for CoastWatch) |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Ritzman et al. 2018, *Harmful Algae* 80:35–45. Interviews with 36 people in Crescent City, CA and Long Beach, WA. | The hardship spread past fishing into hospitality and retail. People faced big barriers to getting financial and employment help during long closures. Abstract only. |
| S2 | Moore S.K. et al. 2020, *Harmful Algae* 96:101799. 2017 mail and online survey in 16 communities, 7 of them in California. | 84% of participants were negatively affected. | S3 | Ekstrom, Moore & Klinger 2020, *Harmful Algae* 94:101740. Fishers trust **professional colleagues and friends** most, then **Sea Grant extension and county staff**, and trust **environmental groups** least. Because of the delay, fishers lost revenue in **non-crab fisheries equal in size to the crab loss**. Disaster aid took more than three years to arrive. | S6 | Moore S.K. et al. 2019, *Marine Policy*. HAB closure index. | Crescent City, Fort Bragg and Moss Landing are the most vulnerable California communities. Closure records had to be **assembled by hand from agency records and archived press releases**, and were complete for California only from 2009. |
| S7 | Free, Moore & Trainer, *Harmful Algae* (≈2022) [UNVERIFIED year] | The protocol in all three states: an area opens if **all 6 crabs** test below the action level (20 ppm meat or 30 ppm viscera). A closure lifts only after **two successive clean surveys at least 7 days apart**. California set biotoxin zones in Oct 2020 and allowed evisceration orders through SB 80 (2021). California **stops sampling a site after one clean pre-season test** and has never started mid-season sampling. Clear zones "promote predictable management." |

There is social pressure not to donate test samples. Rock crab closed abruptly in 2015 about six months after a viscera advisory. "They don't give us a 30-day notice." BMAs often cover two counties. | S12 | CDFW Health Advisories and Closures page (fetched 2026-10-12) | The authoritative status page. CDPH monitors, OEHHA recommends under FGC §5523, and CDFW implements. It lists phone lines and the current closures (snapshot in section 2.3). |
| S14 | SCCOOS CA HAB Bulletin page | An "experimental product" covering C-HARM probabilities, HABMAP nearshore data, strandings and advisories. **The latest issue listed is "May & June 2026"** as of 2026-10-12. | S15 | NOAA 2020 national saltwater angler survey (secondary summary) | The most used and trusted sources are friends and family, bait and tackle shops, and agency websites or regulation guides. [E-adj, not CA-specific] |

## 2. Who decides what, and where official information lives

### 2.1 Authority chain (evidence)
- **CDPH** monitors through its Marine Biotoxin Monitoring Program, which depends mainly on volunteer fishermen and researchers to collect samples. CDPH issues consumption advisories, sets the annual sport-harvested mussel quarantine, and issues commercial evisceration orders [S12, S3, S27].
- **OEHHA** recommends delays, closures and reopenings under FGC §5523, in consultation with the State Public Health Officer [S12, S13].
- **CDFW** Director implements delays and closures by declaration and press release. The Director must give at least 72 hours' notice before trap gear may be set, and runs fair-start rules (FGC §8279.1: 30 days) [S13].
- **Commercial Dungeness crab:**
  - Action levels are 20 ppm DA in meat and 30 ppm in viscera.
  - The sample is six crab per site, and **all six** must pass.
  - Lifting a closure needs **two consecutive clean sets at least 7 days apart** [S7; S26 E-adj].
  - Biotoxin Management Areas have existed since 2020–21. Sources disagree on the count: 9 [S32 UNVERIFIED] versus "a refined set of eight" [S8].
  - Evisceration orders are possible since 2021 [S7, S8].
  - Pre-season testing is required about a month before the opener [S8].
  - **Separate gates** also control the opener: meat-fill quality (Tri-State testing, 24% recovery [S32 UNVERIFIED]) and RAMP whale and turtle entanglement rules [S13].
- **Season anchors:**
  - Central Dungeness opens as early as mid-November.
  - Northern Dungeness opens December 1 [S5].
  - The mussel quarantine typically runs May 1–Oct 31, covers sport harvest only, and does not apply to commercially certified shellfish [S21].
  - Razor clams follow 14 CCR §29.45: limit of 20, the first 20 dug must be kept, and Clam Beach and Del Norte segments alternate by odd and even years [S22].

## 3. Stakeholder profiles

### 3.1 Commercial fishermen (Dungeness and rock crab, market squid, salmon, groundfish, anchovy/sardine)

**Top questions (ranked)**
1. **"Is my area open, closed, delayed, or under an evisceration order right now, and since when?"**
   - [E] Fishers report confusion, and they track area-based openings across BMAs and fair-start rules [S3, S8, S13].
2. **"When will it open, and how much notice will I get?"**
   - [E] Openers decide the season because catch is concentrated in the first six weeks [S5]. "They don't give us a 30-day notice" [S8]. Agencies give at least 72 hours before gear setting [S13].
3. **"What did the latest test show for my port and BMA, and how many clean rounds are still needed?"**
   - [E] Reopening needs two clean sets at least 7 days apart [S7]. Fishers want transparency about the evidence and process [S8].
4. **"Is the toxin trending up or down, and is a bloom coming?" (early warning)**
   - [E] Advance notice lets industry prepare [S11, S5]. Washington's early alert was well received [S3].
   - [A] Fishers will treat model forecasts as background context, not as a decision.
5. **"Which other fisheries and areas can I switch to?"**
   - [E] Fishers lost equal revenue in non-crab fisheries [S5]. Mobile fishers moved to open areas [S8]. Diversification helped [S4]. Roughly two-thirds of vessels stopped fishing temporarily [S31, secondary].
6. **"Can I sell it, and to whom?" (evisceration, buyer capacity, market perception)**
   - [E] Evisceration orders favour the few authorized processors. Some markets reject eviscerated crab, and consumers become confused [S7, S8].
7. **"Are there other gates: meat fill, whale/RAMP, salmon in-season closures?"**
   - [E] Multiple regimes apply at once [S13, S29].

**Current sources and their failure points**
- **CDFW page, news emails and hotline**
  - They are authoritative, but they are fragmented across "various websites," email sign-ups and recorded messages [S8] [E].
  - Boundaries are written as latitudes and landmarks [S12, S13] [E]. Turning them into "my grounds" is hard [A].
- **CDPH result PDFs.** These hold the raw evidence, but in a static format with no alerts [A].
- **Dock talk and colleagues.** This is the most trusted source [S3]. It is fast but can spread rumour [A].
- **TV news.** Widely used but low-trust, and not BMA-specific [S3] [E].
- **Trust deficit.** Californian fishers rated management effectiveness lowest of any group. They perceive thresholds as arbitrary and political [S3] and see the process as non-transparent [S8] [E].
  - **Implication:** an app that looks like *another* authority will inherit that distrust. An app that **shows the agency's own words, dates and data** can lower the cost of verification [A, to test].

**Decision timing**
- **Pre-season (Oct–Nov):** Testing happens about 4 weeks before openers [S8]. Fishers decide on gear, crew, buyer agreements and whether to travel to another BMA [A].
- **Days before the opener:** The gear-setting window is announced at least 72 hours ahead, and fair-start rules bind for 30 days [S13] [E].
- **Mid-season:** Closures can be sudden. In 2015, Washington gave crabbers 3 days to recover 500 traps [S3]. California has no routine mid-season DA sampling [S7] [E].
- **Day trips (squid, wetfish, groundfish):** Decisions are made the night before or the same morning, from weather plus notices [A].

**What would reduce time-to-trustworthy-information**
- [E-backed] **One place** that consolidates *official* notices by area with dates. Fishers' complaint is fragmentation [S8].
- [E] Plain-language **"what changed and when"** plus the link to the source. Fishers want easily understood notifications [S8] and transparency [S3, S8].
- [E] **Test-progress view per BMA.** For example: "Round 1 clean on 10/28; a second clean round ≥7 days later is required by protocol before OEHHA can recommend opening." Shown as protocol, never as a prediction [S7].
- [A] Push alerts for a saved BMA or port.
- [A] Offline and low-bandwidth access at the dock and at sea.

**Evidence vs assumption summary:** Questions 1–3 and 5–6 are **[E]**. Question 4 (forecasts) is **[E]** for wanting advance notice but **[A]** for *how* model forecasts would be used. App and push preferences are **[A]**.

### 3.2 Recreational anglers and CPFV (party-boat) operators

**Top questions (ranked)**
1. **"Is anything I'll catch or keep under a health advisory or closure (crab viscera, anchovy for food, lobster, bivalves)?"**
   - [E] California uses advisories alongside open recreational seasons [S3, S12].
   - [A] Operators need to brief passengers on what they can keep and eat.
2. **"Where is the fish, and what are conditions (swell, wind, water temp, colour, bait)?"**
   - [E-adj] Fishers rely on real-time data [S10].
   - [A] For CPFVs, these trip-planning questions dominate, and HAB is a secondary concern.
3. **"Is the water discoloured or are sea lions sick? Is that a HAB, and does it matter for my fish?"**
   - [E] DA events in Southern California show up as sea-lion strandings [S14].
   - [A] Anglers will read visible red tide or discolouration as "toxic."
4. **"What regulations apply (season, depth, MPA, limits)?"**
   - [E] CDFW provides regulation booklets and a sport-fishing web map [S15 context].
5. **"Is fish from this pier or harbour safe to eat in general?"**
   - [E] Southern California has *non-HAB* contaminant advisories, for example the Palos Verdes Shelf DDT/PCB white croaker advisory [S30].
   - [A] Anglers may conflate the two kinds of advisory.

**Sources and failure points**
- [E-adj] Friends and family, bait and tackle shops, and agency websites are the most used and trusted [S15].
- [A] CPFV operators rely on landing-office fish counts, the NOAA marine forecast, buoy and swell apps, SST and chlorophyll chart services, and radio chatter.
- Failure point:
  - [A] HAB notices sit on a separate CDFW/CDPH page that anglers don't check.
  - [E-adj] Chlorophyll and SST products sold for *finding fish* are visually similar to HAB products, which creates a misinterpretation risk.

**Decision timing:** [A] trip or no-trip is decided the day before and confirmed the same morning. Passenger briefings happen at the dock. Recreational crab and lobster openers are seasonal and well publicized.

**What would reduce time:**
- [A] A one-screen "anything official for my port or area this week?" view with agency links.
- [A] Passenger-ready plain-language text quoting the agency.
- [E] Clear separation of HAB advisories from contaminant advisories (OEHHA) [S30].

**Evidence vs assumption:** This group is largely **[A]**. There is **no California CPFV information-use study** (the last fleet-wide CPFV survey reportedly dates to 2000 [UNVERIFIED]). This is a priority group for interviews.

### 3.3 Recreational shellfish harvesters (razor clams in Humboldt and Del Norte, mussels, other bivalves)

**Top questions (ranked)**
1. **"Can I dig or pick here this weekend, and is it safe to eat?"**
   - [E] Razor clam closures and reopenings are announced by CDFW and CDPH. Del Norte reopened and then closed again within about two months in 2026 [S12, S23].
2. **"Which beach or county, and which toxin (DA vs PSP)?"**
   - [E] Overlapping advisories exist, for example the Del Norte PSP advisory alongside the DA closure [S23]. The quarantine covers sport-harvested mussels only [S21].
3. **"Does cooking or cleaning make it safe?"**
   - [E] Cooking does not destroy DA or PSP toxins [S21, S12].
4. **"Is this year's segment open (odd or even year)?"**
   - [E] Clam Beach and Del Norte segments alternate by year [S22].
5. **"When might it reopen?"**
   - [E-adj] In Washington, digs are short and frequently cancelled, and early warning helps people avoid cancelled travel [S9].

**Sources and failure points**
- [E] CDFW and CDPH notices plus hotlines [S12].
- [E-adj] In Washington, people learn about digs from WDFW notices, and cancellations happen at short notice [S9, S3]. In Washington in 2015, beaches were closed **the day after** an opener, and people were told not to eat clams already dug [S3].
- [A] Local tackle shops, Facebook groups and word of mouth.
- [A] Visiting harvesters, who are tourists, are least likely to see notices.

**Decision timing:** [E-adj] trips are planned days to weeks ahead around low tides, which is when early warning has value for travel and local businesses [S9]. The final go or no-go is on the day [A].

**What would reduce time:**
- [A] A beach- and county-level status card covering both DA and PSP, with the agency source and "as of" date, and the cooking warning.
- [A] A tide-window context that never implies the beach is open.

**Evidence vs assumption:** Risk facts are **[E]**. Behaviour evidence is **[E-adj]** from Washington [S9, S3]. California-specific harvester behaviour is **[A]**: I found no California survey on harvester awareness of the quarantine or signage.

### 3.4 Port managers and harbor districts

**Top questions (ranked)**
1. **"Will the fleet be working (opener delays or closures), and for how long?"**
   - [E] Closures affect harbor revenue and infrastructure upkeep. Slip-fee deferrals were requested, and Sonoma County supported Bodega Bay fishers [S8].
2. **"What do I tell tenants, the public, visitors and restaurants?"**
   - [E] Impacts spread to hospitality and retail [S1, S2]. Consumers are confused about where to buy safe crab [S8].
   - [A] Harbor offices field public calls.
3. **"What relief or programmes exist, and when?"**
   - [E] Disaster aid takes years [S2, S5].
4. **"Are there sick or stranded marine mammals or birds on harbor property?"**
   - [A] For Southern California this follows from strandings data [S14].

**Sources and failure points:**
- [E] Port managers were informal informants in S8, but **no study documents their information workflow**.
- [A] They rely on CDFW notices, associations, the Dungeness Crab Task Force and dock talk.

**Decision timing:** [A] weekly to monthly: staffing, fee policy, ice and fuel orders, public messaging. Longer-horizon planning happens around the opener.

**What would reduce time:**
- [A] A shareable, printable "official status for our port's BMA(s)" card with links and an "as of" stamp, for posting at the harbor office.
- [A] Historical closure-day counts for budgeting.
- [E] Closure history is hard to assemble [S6].

**Evidence vs assumption:** Their needs are **mostly [A]**, apart from the economic-impact evidence [S1, S2, S8].

### 3.6 HAB scientists and managers (CA HAB Bulletin authors, CalHABMAP, C-HARM users, CDPH/OEHHA/CDFW staff)

**Top questions:**
1. "Where is *Pseudo-nitzschia* or DA rising, nearshore versus offshore?"
   - [E] [S14, S17]
2. "Does model risk agree with pier and shore observations?"
   - [E] The SCCOOS page says HABMAP nearshore data "do not always match" C-HARM [S14]. Bivalve toxicity is often decoupled [S18].
3. "How old is each data layer, and what is its provenance?"
   - [E] The Bulletin is experimental and lags. A retrospective synthesis had a 1–2 month delay [S18 UNVERIFIED]. The latest listed issue is May–June 2026 [S14].
4. "Which shellfish or crab sampling should we trigger?"
   - [E] California has no mid-season triggers [S7]. Sampling depends on volunteers [S3, S8].
5. "How do we communicate to the public without over-alarming?"
   - [E] Advisories can drive broad seafood rejection [S8]. There were market impacts [S3, S35].

**Sources and failure points:** C-HARM (CoastWatch/CeNCOOS), HABMAP/ERDDAP, strandings and CDPH data.
- [E] These are not integrated into decisions [S3]. Offshore initiation evades monitoring [S17].

**What would reduce time:**
- [A] A side-by-side view of model versus nearshore observation with explicit timestamps and layer provenance, exportable for bulletins.
- [E-adj] Forecast products are valued when they are timely and decision-scaled [S10, S28].

**Evidence vs assumption:** Problems are **[E]**. Specific UI needs are **[A]**. There is **no published C-HARM user evaluation**.

## 4. User journeys (realistic, October–December 2026)

Each journey lists current steps, pain points (cited where possible), and where CoastWatch could save time **without overstepping official authority**. Here "CW" means CoastWatch.

### J1. Crescent City Dungeness crabber, the week before the Dec 1 opener during DA testing
**Context.** Crescent City is among the most HAB-vulnerable CA communities [S6] and was a Ritzman et al. interview site [S1]. BMA testing happens about a month before the opener [S8]. The 2025–26 northern opener was split: Jan 15, 2026, with a delayed area between Reading Rock and Cape Mendocino [S13].

**Steps today**
1. Hears at the dock or by text that "Trinidad crabs came back hot" [A; colleagues are the most trusted source, S3].
2. Checks the CDFW Health Advisories page and the CDFW news email for the BMA status [S12].
3. Opens the CDPH crab DA results PDF and scans rows for Crescent City and Trinidad: dates and viscera ppm [S27].
4. Works out from the protocol: one crab over 30 ppm viscera means the area stays closed until two clean rounds at least 7 days apart [S7]. They estimate the earliest possible date themselves [A].
5. Calls the CDFW DA line (831) 649-2883 to confirm [S12].
6. Checks the meat-fill (Tri-State) result and whale/RAMP status separately [S13].
7. Decides: bait, crew, buyer, and whether to move to an open BMA, which triggers fair start (30 days) [S13].

**Pain:** fragmentation [S8], non-transparent reasoning [S8], political-boundary perception [S3], and costly misjudgement because catch is concentrated in the first six weeks [S5].

**Where CW helps (inside its lane)**
- One card per saved BMA:
  - The official status in the agency's words.

## 5. Problem statements and design implications

### 5.1 Evidence-based problem statements
- **P1. Fragmentation.** Official HAB information for California fisheries is spread across CDFW pages, email releases, CDPH PDFs, advisory maps and two hotlines. Fishers ask for a "timely, accessible, easily understood notification system" [S8, S12].
- **P2. Low trust; transparency deficit.** California fishers rated HAB management least effective, see thresholds and boundaries as arbitrary, and say decisions are not transparent [S3, S8].
  - Information that *shows the evidence and process*, rather than adding a new voice, is what is missing.
- **P3. Timing is money.** Catch is concentrated in the first six weeks after an opener [S5]. Closures can be sudden with little notice [S3, S8]. Advance notice is a stated management goal [S11].
  - Users need to see status changes fast and understand which protocol steps remain.
- **P4. Layered, easily confused regimes.** Open-on-advisory versus closed, recreational versus commercial, DA versus PSP, HAB versus contaminant advisories, and DA versus meat fill versus whale rules. This layering produced confusion and distrust in 2015 [S3, S13, S23, S30].
- **P5. Absence of data is not absence of risk.** Sampling depends on volunteers, California stops after one clean pre-season test, and there is no mid-season trigger [S3, S7, S8].
  - "No recent result" must never read as "clean."
- **P6. Science products are not decision products.** C-HARM is a probability, is not integrated into CDPH decisions, and diverges from nearshore and bivalve observations [S3, S14, S18].
  - The Bulletin lags months [S14].
- **P7. Reversals and staleness.** Status flips within weeks: Monterey anchovy in June, July and September 2026, and Del Norte razor clams in August and October 2026 [S12, S23, S24].
  - Older notices surface first in search [A].
- **P8. Researchers lack a structured closure history** [S6].
- **P9. Collateral and market harm from over-broad messaging.** Advisories lead consumers to reject seafood broadly. Hospitality and retail suffer [S1, S2, S8].

### 5.2 Coastal/Fishing mode (simple) vs Research mode (detailed)

| Dimension | **Coastal/Fishing mode** | **Research mode** |
|---|---|---|
| Primary question | "Is there an official notice for my species, in my waters, now, and where is the source?" (P1, P4) | "What do observations, models and official actions show, with what provenance and latency?" (P6, P8) |
| Entry point | Saved port, BMA or beach plus species. Location-first; no map literacy assumed [A]. | Map and time-series workspace. Layer catalogue. Registry table. |
| Status display | The agency's verbatim status line, effective date, issuing agency, links (notice, CDPH data, hotline), and "last checked by CW" time. **The latest action first, with "replaces …" for reversals (P7).** | Full notice history with boundaries, transcription metadata and review state. Diffs between versions. |
| Testing evidence | "Testing so far" for the BMA or port, with the protocol explained and remaining steps shown as rules, **not predictions** (P2, P3, P5). A stale-sample banner. | Raw transcribed CDPH results with per-crab values, an exportable CSV, and links to the source PDFs. |
| Science layers | Hidden by default. Optional "ocean context" with a fixed caption: model probability or satellite observation, not toxin in seafood, not used for openings (P6). | All layers (C-HARM, satellite chl, HF radar, HABMAP) with timestamps, version, latency and skill notes. Model vs pier comparison. Same-pipeline metrics only. |
| Alerts | Push or SMS on any official change for saved areas and species [A]. Quiet otherwise. | Data-arrival and registry-change webhooks or feeds [A]. |
| Separation of regimes | Separate rows for DA, PSP, contaminant advisories (link out), meat fill, whale and RAMP (link out). Never merged into one colour (P4). | Same, plus the regime metadata. |
| Language and format | Short, plain language, large type, offline cache, printable card for harbor offices and passenger briefings [A]. Spanish and other languages to be evaluated [A]. | Technical vocabulary. Citations. Export. |
| Tone | No colour-coded "safe/go" semantics. Neutral styling for "no notice found" (P5). | Same constraint. |

**Design implications (cross-cutting)**
1. **Quote, link, timestamp.** Every status statement carries the issuing agency, the effective date, a source link and CW's last-checked time. CW's role is to *find and show* official actions, not to *decide* (P1, P2).
2. **Default to the newest official action, and show reversals explicitly** (P7).
3. **Never use green.** Use "No official notice found in CoastWatch's registry as of [time]," plus "Registry may be incomplete; confirm with CDFW (831) 649-2883 / CDPH (800) 553-4133" (P5).
4. **Explain the protocol, not the outcome** (P2, P3).
5. **Keep forecasts and observations out of the status line** (P6; project rule).
6. **Show sample recency.** Flag when the latest CDPH sample for an area is older than a threshold (P5).
7. **Separate regimes visually and textually** (P4).
8. **Give the research registry durable provenance:** archive copy, transcription date, human-review state. Never auto-mark anything verified, lifted or expired (project rule; P8).
9. **Avoid broad-brush public messaging** that could stigmatize all seafood. Scope everything to species, area and harvest type (P9).

## 6. Interview scripts (30 minutes, semi-structured)

**Common opening (all groups, about 3 minutes)**
> "Thanks for making time. I'm [name] with CoastWatch, an independent research project building a tool that collects official California harmful-algal-bloom notices and ocean data in one place. We are **not** a government agency and we don't make or enforce rules. I'd like to learn how you find and use this kind of information today. There are no right answers. You can skip any question or stop at any time. With your permission I'd like to [audio-record / take notes]. We won't use your name or vessel name in anything we publish, and we won't ask where you fish exactly. Is that OK?"

Confirm consent (section 9). Record role, home port, years of experience and fisheries or activities, in broad categories only.

**Common close (about 3 minutes)**
- "If you could change one thing about how you get this information, what would it be?"
- "Is there anyone else we should talk to?" This is snowball recruitment through *their* choice. Do not ask for contact details of others without their consent.
- "Can we follow up with a short test of a prototype?" Thank them and explain the incentive.

### 6.1 Commercial fishermen (crab, wetfish, squid, salmon, groundfish)
1. **Warm-up (3 min).** "Walk me through your year. Which fisheries, which months?"
2. **Last event (7 min).** "Think about the last time a domoic acid or shellfish-toxin decision affected you, like a delay, closure, advisory or evisceration order. Tell me the story from the first moment you heard about it."
   - Probes: Who or what told you first? How did you check whether it was true? How long until you were confident? What did you do next, and when?
3. **Sources (5 min).** "What do you check, and in what order, for: (a) whether your area is open, (b) test results, (c) weather and ocean?" Ask them to show or describe their phone home screen, bookmarks and contacts if comfortable.
   - Probes: CDFW page, emails, hotline, CDPH PDFs, association, buyer, Task Force, Sea Grant, radio, TV, apps.
4. **Trust (4 min).** "Who do you trust most on this, and why? Has a source ever been wrong or late?" Probe on boundaries, thresholds and transparency without leading.
5. **Timing (4 min).** "How much notice do you need to change plans: gear, crew, buyer, moving ports? What's the latest notice that's still useful?"
6. **Testing and process (3 min).** "How well do you understand how an area gets opened again after a bad test? What's unclear?" Note misconceptions only; do not correct them until the debrief.
7. **Concept probe (3 min, show a paper card).** Show a status card: "Official status: Delayed by CDFW 11/20 · Testing so far: Round 1 (10/28) one crab over the action level · Protocol requires two clean rounds ≥7 days apart · Source links." Ask: "What does this tell you? What would you do? What's missing? Anything that worries you?" *Key check: do they read it as a prediction or as a CW decision?*

### 6.2 Recreational anglers, CPFV operators and shellfish harvesters
1. **Warm-up.** "What do you fish, dig or gather, where in general terms, and how often? (For CPFV: trips per week, typical passengers.)"
2. **Trip planning (6 min).** "Walk me through planning your last trip, from the first idea to leaving the dock or parking lot." Probe tools: tides, weather, apps, fish reports, regulations, HAB notices.
3. **Last advisory (6 min).** "Have you ever changed plans, or wondered whether to, because of a shellfish or fish warning, red water or sick sea lions? What happened?"
4. **Understanding (5 min).** Use neutral comprehension probes:
   - "What does a mussel quarantine mean to you?"
   - "If crab is under an advisory but the season is open, what does that mean?"
   - "Does cooking change anything?"
   - Record the answers and do not correct them until the debrief, when you provide the official facts.
5. **CPFV-specific (4 min).** "What do passengers ask you about eating their catch? Where do you get answers? How do you brief them?"
6. **Harvester-specific (4 min).** "How do you find out if a beach is open or closed? Have you ever arrived to find it different from what you expected?"
7. **Concept probe (3 min).** Show a beach or port card, including a "No official notice found as of…" card. Ask: "Is it safe to eat? Is it legal to go?" *Key safety check: does the participant infer safety or legality?*

### 6.3 Port managers and harbor districts
1. **Role (3 min).** "What does your office deal with when a fishery is delayed or closed?"
2. **Last event (7 min).** "Tell me about the last HAB-related delay or closure and how it reached your office. Who called you, and what did they ask?"
3. **Information flow (6 min).** "Where do you get the official status? Do you redistribute it to tenants, the public or the media? How: postings, email, social media?"
4. **Decisions (5 min).** "What decisions depend on it: fees, staffing, ice and fuel, events, public messaging? How far ahead do you need to know?"
5. **History and records (3 min).** "Do you track closure days or impacts for budgets or grant applications? From what sources?"
6. **Concept probe (3 min).** A printable "official notices for our waters" card. Ask: "Would you post this? What would make you uncomfortable posting it, and what would it need?"

### 6.4 Researchers, HAB scientists and managers
1. **Role and products (3 min).** "Which HAB data or products do you produce or use? For whom?"
2. **Last analysis or bulletin (7 min).** "Walk me through the last time you assembled a HAB picture: the sources, the steps, the time it took, and the pain points."
3. **Data trust (5 min).** "How do you judge the age, provenance and skill of a layer? Where have model and nearshore observations disagreed, and how did you communicate that?"
4. **Closure records (4 min).** "When you need official closure history, how do you get it? What's missing (dates, boundaries, versions)?"
5. **Communication (4 min).** "What misinterpretations of HAB maps or forecasts have you seen from the public, industry or media?" *This is a key input to section 10.*
6. **Concept probe (4 min).** A registry table and a layer panel with provenance. Ask: "Would you cite this? What metadata is mandatory? What would make you distrust it?"

## 8. Moderated usability study protocol

**Design.**
- Moderated, think-aloud, 45–60 minutes. In person at harbors where possible, otherwise remote on the participant's own phone [A: mobile-first].
- **5–8 participants per group** (commercial, recreational and shellfish, port, research), giving 20–32 in total.
- Use a **frozen test build** with seeded, clearly labelled realistic notices, so results are reproducible and no live official status is misrepresented.
- Tasks are counterbalanced.

### 8.1 Tasks

**Commercial (Fishing mode)**
- **C1.** "Find whether any official notice applies to **rock crab** in your waters **this week**, and open the issuing agency's source." (Morro Bay or Crescent City seed.)
- **C2.** "Your BMA had a bad test on Oct 28. Find what the official protocol requires before it can reopen, and the test data source." *Success = states the two-clean-rounds rule and opens the CDPH source. Critical error = states a predicted opening date as fact.*
- **C3.** "Set up alerts so you'd hear about any official change for Dungeness crab in your area."
- **C4.** "Is market squid affected by the Monterey anchovy closure?" *Success = "no official notice found as of [date]; confirm with CDFW"; locates the anchovy notice. Critical error = "squid is safe/open".*

**Recreational / shellfish**
- **R1.** "Can you dig razor clams in Del Norte this weekend? Show the agency source." (Seed: reopened in August, closed Oct 9.) *Critical error = uses the August reopening.*
- **R2.** "Your passenger asks if they can eat the rock crab they caught near the Channel Islands. Find the official advice and source."
- **R3.** Comprehension: "Looking at this 'No official notice found' card, what does it mean?" *Critical error = infers safe or legal.*

**Port**
- **P1.** "Produce something you could post at the harbor office summarizing current official notices for your waters."
- **P2.** "Find how many days the commercial Dungeness fishery in your BMA was delayed last season, with the source."

**Research (Research mode)**
- **S1.** "Compare the latest C-HARM DA probability with the most recent HABMAP observation at [pier], and report each one's timestamp."
- **S2.** "Export the official notices for anchovy in Monterey Bay in 2026 with source links and transcription dates."
- **S3.** "Identify which layers are model output versus observation, and their latency."

### 8.2 Metrics
| Metric | Definition | Target (to calibrate after pilot) |
|---|---|---|
| Task success | Full / partial / fail against a written rubric | ≥80% full success per core task |
| **Time to official source** | Seconds from task start to opening the agency's own page or PDF | Median ≤60 s for C1/R1 [A, baseline from pilot with the status quo: participants do the same task with their usual tools] |
| Errors | Wrong area, wrong species, stale notice used, wrong toxin, navigation dead-ends | Track counts by type |
| **Critical misinterpretations** | Infers safety, legality, productivity or an opening date from CW; treats model or chlorophyll as toxin; treats CW as the issuing authority | **Target 0.** Any occurrence triggers a design fix before release |
| Confidence | 1–7 "How confident are you in your answer?" after each task. Compare with correctness to detect over-confidence. | Calibrated: high confidence only when correct |
| SEQ | Single Ease Question after each task | ≥5.5 |
| SUS | System Usability Scale at the end | ≥68 (industry average), aim ≥75 |
| Trust probe | "Would you rely on this to decide whether to fish or eat? Why?" Coded qualitatively. | Desired: "I'd use it to find the official source quickly," not "I'd rely on it instead of CDFW" |

**Baseline arm.** Have participants do C1 and R1 **with their current tools** first (CDFW site, phone or search) to measure status-quo time-to-source. This directly tests the "time-to-trustworthy-information" hypothesis.

**Analysis.** Rainbow-sheet coding of issues by severity (critical, major, minor). Report each group separately. Five per group finds most usability issues but is not statistically powered. Report counts, not percentages, when n<8.

## 9. Consent and ethics notes

- **Voluntary and informed.**
  - Use a plain-language consent sheet covering: purpose, that CW is **not an agency**, that participation will not affect permits, licences or enforcement, the right to skip or stop, and the recording choice (audio, notes only, or none).
  - Explain how data are stored, for how long, and who sees them.
- **Minimize sensitive data.**
  - Do not collect licence or permit numbers, vessel IDs, exact fishing locations or catch values. Fishing grounds are commercially sensitive [A].
  - Use broad port regions in reports.
- **Agency staff** participate in a professional capacity, with no implication of endorsement. Respect public-records considerations and their organization's policies.
- **Tribal participants and knowledge.** Engage only through tribal governments' own research-consent processes. Do not publish cultural harvest locations.
- **Incentives** must not be coercive. Pay for time regardless of completion.
- **Safety and debrief.** If a participant expresses a dangerous misconception during a session, for example "cooking makes clams safe" or "it's open if the app shows no notice," **correct it in the debrief** and give the official hotlines. Do not leave a participant misinformed for research's sake.
- **Recording of screens:** use the participant's own phone only with consent, and hide notifications.
- **Ethics review:** if run with or through a university or Sea Grant partner, obtain IRB review or an exemption determination before any contact [A: likely exempt category, but the determination must come from the IRB].
- **No claims of research participation** in marketing. Report findings as "interviews with N participants," with dates and methods.

## 10. Things CoastWatch must NEVER imply (with the evidence behind each)

| # | Never imply… | Why (evidence of misinterpretation or harm risk) |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | **That an area or species is open, legal or permitted to fish or harvest.** | Legality depends on multiple overlapping regimes: DA closures, meat fill, RAMP whale rules, fair start, gear-setting windows, seasons, MPAs and odd/even-year beach segments [S13, S22]. Layered regimes caused confusion and distrust in 2015 [S3]. |
| N2 | **That seafood is safe to eat** (including "no notice = safe"). | Recreational crab can be open *on advisory* not to eat viscera [S3, S12]. Cooking does not destroy toxins [S21]. Sampling is volunteer-dependent and California stops after one clean pre-season test, with no mid-season trigger [S3, S7, S8]. |
| N3 | **That absence of a notice or sample means absence of toxin.** | Same as N2. Some blooms begin offshore and evade monitoring [S17]. |
| N4 | **That chlorophyll, satellite colour or a C-HARM probability is a toxin level, a closure or an official forecast.** | C-HARM gives probabilities with stated thresholds. Nearshore data "do not always match," and bivalve toxicity is often decoupled [S14, S18]. C-HARM is not integrated into CDPH decisions [S3]. Project rule: never present chlorophyll as toxin. |
| N5 | **That an observation is a forecast, or that a model forecast is an observation.** | Project rule. Also, fishers "rely heavily on real-time data" and combine sources nonlinearly [S10], so mislabeled layers propagate into decisions. |
| N6 | **A predicted opening or reopening date, or the likely result of a pending test.** | Openings are OEHHA/CDFW decisions [S12, S13]. Fishers already distrust the process [S3, S8]. A wrong CW prediction would be read as an official signal and cost money in a six-week season [S5]. |
| N7 | **That CoastWatch is, or speaks for, CDFW, CDPH, OEHHA or NOAA**, or that a record is human-verified, lifted or expired unless a person ran the review. | Trust and authority confusion [S3]. Project rule on official notices. |
| N8 | **That fishing will be good or productive** (hotspots from chlorophyll or SST, "good digging day"). | Productivity framing turns a safety and information tool into a lure next to closed or advisory areas [A]. It invites conflating fish-finding charts with HAB layers [A]. |
| N9 | **That a whole species, port or the coast's seafood is unsafe** (over-broad warnings). | Advisories already cause broad consumer rejection, market harm and hospitality losses [S1, S2, S8, S35]. Scope must be species × area × harvest type. Commercially certified shellfish are outside the sport quarantine [S21]. |
| N10 | **That HAB notices cover chemical contaminants, or vice versa** (e.g., Palos Verdes DDT/PCB advisories). | Different agencies and regimes [S30]. Anglers may conflate them [A]. |
| N11 | **That an older notice is current** (status without an "as of" time, or a stale reopening shown first). | Rapid reversals: Del Norte reopened in August and closed Oct 9, 2026, and Monterey anchovy changed in June, July and September 2026 [S12, S23, S24]. The CA HAB Bulletin's latest issue is months old [S14]. |
| N12 | **Precise boundaries beyond what the agency published** (e.g., snapping a latitude line to a coastline polygon without saying so). | Fishers already contest boundaries [S3]. BMAs span counties [S8]. Transcription errors are possible (see the rock crab coordinate discrepancy in §2.3). Always show the agency's boundary text next to any drawn line. |
| N13 | **That a status covers both DA and PSP** (or other toxins) when it covers only one. | DA closures and PSP advisories coexist [S23]. |
| N14 | **That research model skill means operational reliability.** | Project science rule (same-pipeline numbers only; the PINN matches, does not beat, the ConvLSTM). There is no operational validation against regulatory decisions [S3]. |

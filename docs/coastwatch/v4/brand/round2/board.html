Neritic · brand round 2: Shelf band against Chart label

Owner's brief: do not finalise B yet; refine it against the strongest alternative and show the actual 16/24/32 px results. Every small image below is a real Chromium screenshot at device scale 1 (or 2 where marked). Enlargements are those screenshots scaled by whole numbers with nearest-neighbour, so each square is one screen pixel.

Three changes apply to every round-2 candidate, C2 included: the tile fills the favicon square (round 1 left 1.3 px of margin each side at 16 px); the favicon tile is one step lighter than brand navy (#1b5583), so its edge survives on a dark tab; and the 16 px and 24 px favicons are hand-drawn pixel masters. The SVG serves 32 px and every retina slot. To ship the masters, the site declares favicon.ico (16/24/32/48) and no SVG favicon, because Chrome and Firefox pick an SVG over the ICO when both are offered.

Strongest alternative: C · Chart label

C (Chart label) is the strongest alternative, not A. The favicon is where B is being judged, and A loses there: after four passes its 16 px cut still reads as stripes with a plug, it needs three tints, and in one colour it turns into an H. A is the best illustration of the family, not a better identity. C is the opposite case. Its 16 px tile is the most legible thing on the board, it comes with a complete typographic system (italic for water names, spaced italic capitals for editorial use), and its contour-break logic is real chart practice. Its weakness is what makes it a fair test of B: at tab size C is a letter tile, the same genre as the rejected N tile. So the question for round 2 is whether B can become as legible as C at 16 px without turning into a letter, a road or a slash.

Recommendation

Carry R2 · Bight forward as the B direction; do not finalise it yet. It is the only B variant that stops being a stroke at 16 px. The band no longer runs corner to corner, the two solids are unequal, and in colour nothing about it is a letter, a road or a slash. Among wave, swoosh and letter-tile neighbours it stays findable on both tab colours, and from 24 px up it reads as a coast turning east.

It does not beat C2 on raw 16 px legibility. C2's n is the fastest thing to find in a crowded strip. But it is a letter tile, the genre the owner already rejected, and below 32 px the italic and the contour are gone, leaving a pixel n between two dashes. R2 wins on meaning and on not being a letter. If 16 px legibility must win outright, C2 is the honest pick.

Open issues for one more short round:

  • One colour. Loose, R2's two solids read as a lowercase r. The one-colour version must keep the tile rim (shown in section 3), which turns the shelf into a cut.
  • The pale Bight touches the tile corner, which softens the outline on light tabs and can read as a folded corner.
  • A hand-cut 32 px master to sharpen the point (32 px is still the SVG).
  • Shipping: the masters only appear if the site serves favicon.ico and no SVG favicon.

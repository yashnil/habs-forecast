# Neritic v4 brand concepts: critique

The judgements below are based on `board-desktop.png` and `board-favicons.png` (pixel-exact 16/24/32 px, enlarged 8x). Avoided from earlier rounds: closed loops, coast-cut hooks, the split disc, the N tile, Depth N, Soundings. Wordmark face: **Literata** (SIL OFL 1.1, Google Fonts). It is a screen-tuned serif with an optical-size axis and a true italic, and it is unused in earlier rounds (Sora, Inter, Source Serif 4).

## A · Canyon (reduced contours)
Three depth bands. Both isobaths are cut by one canyon reaching toward the coast.
- **Distinctive:** the most chart-like and Monterey-specific at 128 px and above.
- **16 px:** the weakest, after four passes. Angled tongues read as ticks, tapered ones as a play arrow, doubled ones as a plus. The favicon is a separate drawing (one blunt tongue), and at 16 px it reads as stripes with a plug, not a canyon.
- **Type:** sits well beside upright Literata.
- **Resemblance:** low among marine brands. Large, it can look like a tree or a delta.
- **Scale:** it needs three tints. In mono it becomes an H-like shape.

## B · Shelf band (figure-ground)
Deep ocean and land are two solids. The shelf is the gap between them, never drawn: narrow in the north, swelling at a bay, pinched at a point and widening into the Southern California Bight.
- **Distinctive:** the meaning is exact, because "neritic" is the space between land and deep water. It is not a letter and not a split disc.
- **16 px:** the best. A band about 2 px wide holds on light and dark tabs. Below 32 px the bay and point are lost, but the taper survives.
- **Type:** its slant echoes the serif's stress without imitating a letter.
- **Resemblance:** the risk comes from genres, not marine brands. It can read as a road, a river, a backslash or a stylised N. The dark-ground version (two pale shapes) reads as brackets, so keep the tile.
- **Scale:** excellent. With one colour and two shapes, mono, favicon and embossing are the same drawing.

## C · Chart label (wordmark-led)
Lowercase "neritic" in Literata SemiBold Italic, after the chart convention that water is lettered in sloped type. The symbol is an isobath broken by an italic **n**, as a depth label breaks a contour. Editorial use takes spaced italic capitals, as a chart names a sea.
- **Distinctive:** strong as typography. The contour-break logic is genuinely cartographic.
- **16 px:** the n is crisp, but the contour stubs shrink to one or two pixels. At favicon size it is a letter tile, the same genre as the rejected N tile.
- **Type:** the most coherent by construction. In a header, though, the italic is quieter than the upright versions.
- **Resemblance:** low among marine brands. Italic serif wordmarks are common in publishing.
- **Scale:** clean in mono; the editorial version is the richest.

## Recommendation
**B · Shelf band.** It is the only concept whose 16 px favicon says the same thing as its 512 px icon. It is one colour and two shapes, drawn from real California geometry, and it carries the name's meaning without a letter, wave or dot. Pair it with upright Literata SemiBold. Keep C's conventions as secondary typography: italic for water names, and spaced italic capitals over broken contours for editorial titles. Use A as illustration (report covers, a Monterey page header), not as the identity.

Two open questions for the owner: does B read as a road or a backslash at 16 px, and should the dark-ground variant always be the tile?
